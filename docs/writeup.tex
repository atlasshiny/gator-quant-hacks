\section{Summary}
% ============================================================
% 3--5 sentences: what the strategy does, the core edge, headline results, key caveat.
\textbf{Overall Strategy:} On each ES order-book update, we combine the GA-selected book features and XGBoost output into one standardized score. If it clears a cost-based threshold, then we take a one-contract market position in that direction, then exit after a fixed holding time.
 
We study whether the shape of the ten-level order book of the CME E-mini
S\&P~500 future (ES) predicts the direction of the mid-price over the next
[$h$] seconds by more than a simple top-of-book rule. An XGBoost model
estimates the probabilities of a down move, no move, and an up move from
[25] book-state features; a genetic algorithm (GA), accelerated on GPUs,
searches for the feature subset, entry threshold, and warm-up that turn the model's output into a trading rule. The rule is frozen on in-sample days and replayed once, out of sample, in an event-driven simulator that fills market orders against the recorded order book. Out of sample and net of fees, the strategy achieves [X] trades with a mean net P\&L of \$[Y] per trade (t-statistic [T]), a net Sharpe ratio of [S] and a maximum drawdown of [D]\% over [N] trading days. It [does / does not] beat a random feature subset and a level-1 imbalance rule. The sample covers one instrument and [N] sessions (about [six] months), so we report per-trade statistics, uncertainty around the Sharpe ratio, and sensitivity to latency and costs alongside the headline
numbers.

\section{Economic and Data Hypothesis}
% ============================================================
 
\subsection{Economic Hypothesis}
We expect the shape of the ES ten-level order book (depth imbalance across
levels, depth slope, spread, and recent order flow) to contain information
about the direction of the mid-price over the next [$h$] seconds that a
simple top-of-book rule does not capture. Market makers update quotes with a
delay and must avoid trading against informed flow, so transient book
imbalances are briefly mispriced. Traders who cross the spread without regard
to the state of the book pay for that, and so do market makers whose quotes
are stale. These participants are on the other side of our trades. The edge
persists because supplying liquidity into such imbalances means bearing
adverse-selection risk (some imbalances are informed and continue), which
limits how much capital competes the concession away. We do not impose the
sign of the effect: the model estimates it from the data.
 
\paragraph{Testable prediction.} Net of spread and fees, the frozen rule earns
a positive out-of-sample return; realized forward returns increase across
buckets of the model's directional score; and the rule beats (1) a random
feature subset of the same size and (2) the sign of level-1 imbalance.
 
\paragraph{It fails if.} (1) the out-of-sample net return is not positive
once spread crossing and fees are included; (2) realized returns are not
monotone in the directional score; or (3) the rule does not beat both
baselines. Our strategy trades with marketable orders and therefore pays the
spread it hopes to earn, so (1) is the most likely failure.

\section{Data and Universe}
% ============================================================
% cite this https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.02754
We trade a single instrument: the CME E-mini S\&P~500 future (ES), using
Databento's continuous front-month symbol \texttt{ES.c.0}, which maps to
the March 2024 contract (ESH4) throughout the sample; no contract roll
occurs in-sample and prices are not back-adjusted.

Market data is obtained from Databento~\cite{Databento} (dataset \texttt{GLBX.MDP3}, schema \texttt{mbp-10}), which provides every order-book event with the top ten price levels on each side at nanosecond resolution. The sample covers [Monday 2024-01-08 through Friday 2024-01-12, INSERT ANOTHER DATE], [N COUNT EVENTS] book events.

We use the [\texttt{ts\_recv} / \texttt{ts\_event}] timestamp for all features. Survivorship bias and corporate actions do not apply to a single futures contract. Snapshots with a crossed or locked book are dropped, empty
levels are filled with zero size, and no feature or label spans the daily
17:00--18:00 ET maintenance break.

\section{Methodology}
% ============================================================
\subsection{Model Training}
% Define Genetic Programming + XGBoost
\subsection{Model and Genetic Search}
\paragraph{XGBoost.} A gradient-boosted tree classifier~\cite{chen2016xgboost}
(GPU histogram method, \texttt{multi:softprob}, three classes) outputs
$[P_{\text{sell}},P_{\text{hold}},P_{\text{buy}}]$. Out-of-fold probabilities
are produced with an expanding-window purged walk-forward scheme
\cite{lopezdeprado2018}: validation blocks begin $H$ rows plus an embargo after
the end of the training block, so that overlapping labels do not cross the
boundary. The first block has no out-of-fold prediction and is excluded. The
directional score is
\begin{equation}
    q_i = P_{\text{buy}}(\mathbf{x}_i) - P_{\text{sell}}(\mathbf{x}_i) \in [-1,1],
\end{equation}
and is appended to the feature matrix as one GA input column. The deployed
model is trained once on all in-sample rows.
 
\paragraph{Genome.} Each individual is a 64-bit chromosome
(Table~\ref{tab:genome}). The 28 GA input columns are the snapshot features,
the score $q$, and unused padding columns whose flag bit is held at zero.

\paragraph{Rule and fitness.} Each GA input column is standardized with
in-sample statistics, $z_{i,j} = (u_{i,j}-\mu_j)/(\sigma_j+\varepsilon)$. For a
chromosome $b$ with active set $\mathcal{S}_b$ and threshold $\tau_b$, the
signal and position are
\begin{equation}
    S_i = \sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}_b} z_{i,j}, \qquad
    p_i = \begin{cases} +1 & S_i > \tau_b \\ -1 & S_i < -\tau_b \\ 0 & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases}
\end{equation}
% TODO(verify): the Triton kernel includes the short side (p_i = -1),
% charges costs, and runs on standardized columns.
and the per-event net return is
$\tilde r_{b,i} = p_i\, r_{i\to i+H} - |p_i|\,c_{\text{rt}}$, with
$c_{\text{rt}}$ the measured round-trip cost. A fused Triton
kernel~\cite{tillet2019triton} evaluates the whole population in GPU memory,
accumulating the first four moments of $\tilde r_{b,i}$ in a single pass.
Fitness is a Sharpe-type score penalized for negative skewness and excess
kurtosis,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{F}(b) = \mathrm{SR}_b - 0.5\max(0,-\gamma_{1,b}) - 0.1\max(0,\gamma_{2,b}-3),
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{SR}_b$ is the mean over the standard deviation of $\tilde
r_{b,i}$ (scaled by a fixed constant) and $\gamma_1,\gamma_2$ are skewness and
kurtosis. The score is used only to rank chromosomes; we do not report it as a
performance measure.
 
\paragraph{Evolution.} The population is split into islands, one per GPU
($G=[\,]$ GPUs, total population $P=[\,]$). Each generation keeps the top 10\%
unchanged, draws parents uniformly from that elite group, applies
single-point crossover at a random bit, and flips one random bit in each
offspring with probability 0.10. Every $[m]$ generations the islands exchange
their best chromosomes. We run $T=[\,]$ generations and record the number of
evaluated chromosomes $N_{\text{eval}} = P\cdot T = [\,]$, because a larger
search makes the best in-sample result look better by chance
(Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

\subsection{Adaptive Re-Evolution and Validation}
To address non-stationarity, the GA is re-run on each rolling window, so the active feature set can change as regimes shift. Selection, fitness
evaluation, and model fitting are performed strictly inside a purged
walk-forward scheme: for each fold, evolution and training use data in
$[t_0, t_1)$, and testing uses $[t_1 + e, t_2)$ with an embargo $e \ge h$
bars. No test-period data enters fitness evaluation, hyperparameter choice,
or genome selection. We additionally report [a static-feature XGBoost
baseline, and a random-subset baseline with the same feature count] to
isolate the contribution of the evolutionary search.

\subsection{Strategy}
The \emph{model} (features, XGBoost, GA) produces a frozen rule. The
\emph{strategy} below converts that rule into orders. Everything it needs
(feature mask, threshold, standardization constants, model weights) is stored
in one frozen bundle written from in-sample data and is not updated during
the OOS replay.
 
\subsubsection{Signal Construction}
At every ten-level book update $t$, the strategy computes the snapshot features
$\mathbf{x}_t$ with the same code path as in training, runs the XGBoost model
when the score column is active, standardizes the active columns, and forms
\begin{equation}
    S_t = \sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}} z_{t,j}.
\end{equation}
The strategy waits for a warm-up of $\max(L,\,1001)$ updates, where $L$ is the
GA lookback and 1001 fills the rolling OFI window.
 
\subsubsection{Portfolio Construction}
The portfolio holds one instrument (ESH4) and is long, short, or flat. The
target direction is $+1$ if $S_t>\tau$, $-1$ if $S_t<-\tau$, and no trade
otherwise. Entries are allowed only from a flat position, so exposure never
stacks and holding periods never overlap. No cross-sectional ranking or
neutralization applies. The strategy is flat before the CME daily maintenance
break and at the end of the window, so it holds nothing overnight.
 
\subsubsection{Position Sizing}
Each entry trades a fixed $n=[1]$ contract regardless of $|S_t|$, which keeps
the test about the predictive content of the signal and not about sizing. One
ES contract has a \$50 index multiplier; at an index level near [4{,}800] a
position is roughly [\$240{,}000] of notional, about [0.24$\times$] leverage
on the \$1{,}000{,}000 account.
 
\subsubsection{Rebalancing Frequency}
The strategy is event-driven: features and exit conditions are updated at every
book snapshot, with no time bars. Entries are throttled by a minimum interval
of [500]\,ms between orders and a cooldown of [500]\,ms after each exit. A
position is closed after a fixed holding period $h=[\,]$\,ms, set to the median
wall-clock duration of the label horizon measured in-sample, and is closed
early when the maintenance-break window (16:55--18:05 ET) begins. Exit
conditions are checked at each book update. The feature set, threshold, and
model are not changed during the OOS period.
 
\subsubsection{Execution Assumptions}
Entries and exits are market orders, filled against the displayed depth when
they reach the simulated venue, so spread crossing and depth-based slippage are
embedded in the fills. Orders reach the venue after an assumed latency of
$[\,]$\,ms; Section~\ref{sec:results} reports results for several values.
Passive orders are not simulated, because historical data cannot reveal queue
position, and our own market impact on later book updates is not modeled.
Exchange and brokerage fees of \$2.00 per contract per fill are deducted after
the simulation.
 
\subsection{Backtest Design}
We use NautilusTrader~\cite{nautilus}, an open-source event-driven engine that
replays recorded data in timestamp order, so the strategy sees only
information stamped at or before the current simulation time. Databento
\texttt{mbp-10} records are loaded as ten-level depth snapshots on an L2
market-by-price book, with a netting account and \$1{,}000{,}000 of starting
capital. All fitting and tuning happen outside the engine on in-sample days;
the engine replays the frozen strategy over the OOS window once. The
in-sample window is replayed through the same engine only to measure the
in-sample/out-of-sample gap under identical assumptions. Nautilus's native
statistics and tearsheet are gross of our per-fill fee, so headline metrics come
from our own net calculation. Daily returns are built from realized balance, so
intraday drawdowns are not visible; we therefore also report per-trade
statistics.

\section{Risk Management}
% ============================================================
Price risk is small (at most two ES contracts, about \$500{,}000 notional on \$1{,}000{,}000, held $h$ seconds). The main risks are the 1.32-tick (\$16.50) round-trip spread, informed bursts that continue, thin books and model decay; all limits sit outside the GA.

\subsection{Risk Controls}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Position limit:} at most 2 contracts, one position at a time, each order $\le 10\%$ of the size displayed at the touch.
    \item \textbf{Volatility target:} each trade risks $\le 0.025\%$ of equity; size halves above $1.5\times$ normal 5-minute volatility for that time of day and entries stop above $2.5\times$.
    \item \textbf{Gross / net exposure:} one instrument, so gross equals net; flat before the 16:00 CT break.
    \item \textbf{Drawdown rule:} a 2\% daily loss halts the session; $-2\%$ from peak halves size; $-5\%$ stops trading for five sessions.
    \item \textbf{Execution and model:} trade only if the predicted move is $\ge 1.5\times$ cost; halve size if the last 200 trades net $\le 0$; skip CPI, payroll and FOMC releases, holidays and roll weeks.
\end{itemize}
 
\subsection{Risk Decomposition}
P\&L is split into gross edge, spread, slippage and fees by burst size and time of day (edge should rise with burst size); we report the 97.5\% Expected Shortfall and the breakeven cost.

\section{Limitations and Next Steps}
% ============================================================
\subsection{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{Data:} trained on one month of ES only, which captures a single market regime; MBP-10 has no queue position.
    \item \textbf{Search budget:} compute limited the GA to 100 generations, so fitness may not have converged and results may be a local optimum.
    \item \textbf{Overfitting:} many parameter sets tested on overlapping returns from a short sample, so results may not generalize.
    \item \textbf{Regime risk:} untested under stress; bursts may not revert in high-volatility markets.
    \item \textbf{Execution:} assumes 1.32-tick round trips with no impact or latency model.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Next Steps}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{More data and generations:} ideally train on 2+ years, including 2022 and Aug.\ 2024, and run the GA until fitness plateaus; test on NQ.
    \item \textbf{Validation:} hold out a clean test period and fix overlapping fitness.
    \item \textbf{Execution:} simulate passive fills with MBO data, then paper-trade live in MES.
\end{itemize}
